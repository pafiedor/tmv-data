# Which euro-area issuers publish the order side? — working note

Companion to *Code 12* (The Macro Prudential View). Survey run on the public
documents as of 4 September 2026.

## The question, stated strictly

Does the issuer publish, in a regular public document, the composition **by
investor type of the demand received** in primary issuance — who asked — as
opposed to who was allocated, who holds the stock, or how large the book was?
Italy's Rapporto sul Debito Pubblico chart (monthly, seven investor categories,
dealers' client flow in a window around nominal BTP auctions) is the template.

Three things do **not** count, and all three are published widely: allotment
composition after a syndication; investor-base or creditor-structure statistics;
order-book size, account count or oversubscription ratio without an
investor-type split.

## Documents examined, by issuer

For each issuer the flagship recurring publications were read: the annual report
or review, the funding outlook or programme, the per-syndication results
notices, and the investor presentation where one is published. Quarterly
bulletins, national-language variants and the long tail of deal-by-deal
releases were **not** exhausted. The negatives are strong for the documents an
institutional reader would check first and weaker beyond that.

- **France — AFT.** Syndication results releases of 21 January, 12 February,
  25 March and 29 April 2025 and 14 January, 11 February and 14 April 2026
  (each: total demand as a single figure, then the allocation by investor type,
  hedge funds at 1% in all seven); AFT annual report and monthly bulletin
  (investor-base statistics are holdings).
- **Germany — Finanzagentur.** Transaction Reviews for the syndicated Bund
  issues (order book as a scalar: "Final orderbook closed in excess of € 38
  bn"; an investor-type pie chart with no caption in either language, read
  as allocation by convention only); the creditor-structure table, which the
  Finanzagentur describes as estimated from Bund Issues Auction Group members'
  purchases and sales to end investors — a flow-derived stock. Query with the
  Finanzagentur press office.
- **Spain — Tesoro Público.** The Chart Pack served at the Tesoro's Chart Pack
  address on 4 September 2026 (file dated 9 December 2022; server
  Last-Modified 1 March 2023), whose investor slides are allocation and
  holdings; the Kingdom of Spain investor presentation of December 2023,
  whose investor-base section is holdings; per-syndication allocation figures
  as reproduced in the press. One chart's label does not settle allocation
  versus demand. Query with the Tesoro.
- **Netherlands — DSTA.** Outlook 2026 (Figure 12, "Data on DSL turnover by
  investor type", six dealer-reported categories — secondary turnover);
  syndication results ("In total €32.74 billion in bids has been received in
  the order book… Of this amount 85.77% is allocated to 'real money' accounts
  and 14.23% to 'other' accounts" — the split is of the allocation).
- **Belgium — Debt Agency.** OLO 97 deal summary, January 2023 (order book "in
  excess of EUR 51 billion" from "over 300 investors"; allocation by geography
  and by investor type: fund managers 31%, bank treasuries 31%, central banks
  and other public entities 17%, banks 11%, hedge funds 5%, pension funds 3%,
  insurers 2%); Annual Report (net purchases by investor type on the
  secondary market — 2025: client banks +€12.64bn, fund managers +€2.91bn,
  hedge funds −€3.58bn, "on the secondary market" in the Agency's words).
- **Austria — OeBFA.** Investor presentation and syndication notices ("an
  average oversubscription rate of 8.9 times"; allocation by investor type).
- **Ireland — NTMA.** Syndication results ("The total order book of circa €66
  billion included over 400 individual accounts", then "The final allocation
  was…"); annual report.
- **Portugal — IGCP.** Syndication notices and annual report (order book as a
  scalar; allocation by investor type).
- **Finland — State Treasury.** Syndication notices ("order book of over EUR
  31 billion from 220 investors"; allocation by investor type, coarse).
- **European Union.** Commission press releases for NGEU syndications, June
  2021 onward (order book, then allocation by investor type; "Hedge Funds /
  Other" as a category in some releases).

## What the evidence supports

Among the ten sovereign issuers examined, Italy's is the only periodic
investor-type decomposition of primary-market demand found in these document
sets as of the survey date. That is the sentence; not "no issuer publishes who
asked". The Euro Market Activity Report covers fourteen sovereign markets and
does not name them, so at least four are outside this survey.

## Near misses

Belgium's net purchases by investor type, the Netherlands' DSL turnover by
investor type and Germany's creditor-structure table are all dealer-reported
and all secondary or stock measures with no anchor to the primary market.
Whether any is drawn from the harmonised return rather than from a national
collection is not stated in their documentation. What separates Italy's is the
date window — MEF selects the flows "dal giorno successivo all'annuncio di
un'asta fino al giorno antecedente il regolamento della medesima" — and the
fact that its source is named.
